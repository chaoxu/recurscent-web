Recurscent / identity study 01

One mark, many readings.

A working board for a Japanese-informed identity built from Genji-kō, recursive structure, ascent, and the geometric discipline of family crests.

Design criterion

Each mark holds as a solid one-ink hinata-mon at small scale and opens into motif-specific kamon linework at display sizes.

What changes

From diagram to identity system.

Standalone mark. Each crest survives at 16 px without the wordmark.

Separate wordmark. The English name keeps its own rhythm and generous space.

Kamon construction. A solid hinata-mon silhouette gains fukurin borders, komochi rules, and kage contours at large scale.

Cultural reading note

Genji-kō diagrams traditionally read right to left, counting five incense sticks from the right. House adaptations on this board that rise left to right state that reversal explicitly, and non-Genji crests distinguish historical kamon families from newly composed Recurscent marks.

Hatsune, redrawn as a mon

The five-pillar topology adapts the Hatsune diagram from the Genji-kō incense game into a left-to-right ascending house mark (traditional diagrams read right to left). In linework view, kasane-fukurin inner seams clarify the under-passing bridge while stepped komochi contours and a grounded dai-suji base follow the five ascending pillars.

Genji-guruma, the spoked wheel

Adapted from the classical Genji-guruma carriage-wheel crest family. Eight jointed felloes and tapered primary spokes hold a crisp small-scale silhouette, while display linework adds a taut outer tire rule, grouped tsugite felloe cleats, interrupted koshiki hub collars, and engraved spoke ridges.

Source: Genji-guruma kamon adaptationRole: radial motion + recurrence

Kagaribi, the watch-fire

Inspired by the Kagaribi chapter image of iron flare baskets rather than a canonical Genji-kō glyph. Five fanned brazier ribs support a three-tongued Edo kaen crest flame, resolved in linework view with curved inner kaen contours and an over-under woven kagariko iron basket.

Source: Genji ch. 27 / kaen-mon house markRole: ascent + focal energy

Yūgiri, evening mist

Draws on traditional tsuki-ni-kasumi moon-and-mist crests rather than wavy illustration. Four stepped horizontal suyari-gasumi channels carve five ascending ink strata across a circular disc, extended at large scale by asymmetric kage-gasumi mist fingers, sparse nested strata rules, and a clipped lunar arc.

Source: Yūgiri motif / kasumi-mon adaptationRole: depth + calm authority

Sawarabi, coiled bracken

Translates the Sawarabi chapter motif into the compass-coiled geometry of warabi-guruma bracken crests. Three coiled shoots alternate with three young buds around a ringed hub, detailed at display scale with coiled stem-to-fiddlehead suji, inner bud spirals, and partial dorsal komochi curves.

Source: Sawarabi motif / warabi-mon adaptationRole: renewal + rotational balance

Mizuhiki, interlaced rings

Replaces a loose spiral with a three-ring wa-chigai knot in alternating over-under weave. The quiet mark uses bold single ribbons, while linework view resolves each loop into a five-strand parallel mizuhiki cord bundle with explicit over-under crossings and moto-yui ties.

Source: wa-chigai / mizuhiki house adaptationRole: craft + continuous weave

Ireko-masu, nested measures

Combines a chamfered sumikiri-kaku crest frame with recursively inscribed rotated and upright masu squares. Display linework adds pinwheel kigumi corner joinery, a komochi outer rule, and open nested corner brackets that follow the 45-degree progression.

Source: sumikiri-kaku / mimasu geometryRole: mathematical recursion

Maru ni Hatsune, boundary-resolved

Anchors all five Genji-kō pillars directly into a bold enclosing maru ring, with the fifth ascending chord spanning arc to arc and the paired stems resolving into clean Hatsune bridges. Linework view pairs a single komochi-itowa outer seal rule with uchifukurin seams and capped kage-suji chords resolving all five vertical axes to the inner boundary.

Source: Maru-ni-Genji-kō house adaptationRole: institutional seal + trust

Asa-no-ha inside a kikkō

Sets a six-pointed asa-no-ha star of solid kite facets inside a bold hexagonal kikkō tortoise-shell frame. At large scale, a komochi-kikkō outer rule, four-facet petal veins, and symmetric hagumiko valley triangulation complete the traditional hemp-leaf lattice.

Source: asa-no-ha / kikkō kamon geometryRole: structured growth + precision
Separate logotype

Wordmark studies

Lowercase recurscent carries the name with human rhythm, open counters, and curved joins rather than repeating the crest’s straight-line construction.

Independent selection. The final wordmark can be chosen separately from the symbol. Any crest above can pair with any lowercase direction below, and either element can stand alone.

Font provenance. The selected flared humanist sans uses the Optima / Gill Sans / Avenir Next / Candara platform stack with the locally bundled IBM Plex Sans as fallback. IBM Plex Mono and Inconsolata remain bundled for supporting labels.

Venetian and old-style roman direction

Warm oblique pen stress, cupped baseline serifs, and generous lowercase counters give the ten-letter name an unhurried literary cadence. Teardrop r ears lean toward adjacent bowls without forcing a synthetic ligature.

Direction only: Iowan Old Style, Palatino, Georgia fallback (not bundled)Role: editorial warmth

Selected Recurscent wordmark

The selected lowercase wordmark uses subtly tapered stems and flared stroke endings to bring calligraphic softness into a clean sans silhouette. Open tracking lets the repeated r, c, and e curves breathe beside every crest in the system.

Primary direction: Optima, Gill Sans, Avenir Next, Candara, IBM Plex Sans fallbackRole: selected wordmark

Restrained chancery italic direction

Rooted in Renaissance chancery italics rather than flourishing script. Entry hooks on r, u, and n answer upturned exit strokes on c, e, and t, carrying the eye through a fluid return-and-ascent motion.

Working recommendation

Current identity direction.

  1. Flared humanist sans. The lowercase recurscent wordmark is now the primary name treatment across the identity system.
  2. Hatsune mon. Preserves direct continuity with the deployed Recurscent symbol while gaining fukurin bridge seams and stepped komochi construction at display scale.
  3. Genji-kō mon. Resolves all five pillars into the enclosing maru boundary, giving the Hatsune topology the authority of a formal seal.
  4. Ireko-masu. Expresses recursion directly through inscribed chamfered, rotated, and upright measure frames at every scale.
